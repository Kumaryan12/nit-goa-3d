# Campus bicycles and shared buggies

In **Walk with avatar**, choose **Bicycle** or **Buggy** on open campus ground. Both can travel on roads, paths and clear outdoor ground. The mount search checks your current position, then nearby clear spots within three metres with a walkable approach. If no safe spot fits, the controls explain where to move.

- W / Up: accelerate gradually.
- S / Down: brake; keep holding to reverse a stopped buggy. Bicycles do not reverse.
- A/D or Left/Right: steer. Touch arrows/joystick provide the same controls.
- Space or the Brake button: stop.
- F or On foot: dismount from your own vehicle at a nearby clear position.
- Pause, blur and hidden tabs stop the vehicle immediately.

## Share a four-seat buggy

There is one driver and three passenger seats on two benches. In the signed-in live campus, walk within four metres of a stopped buggy while on foot. The Walk controls show **Hop into [name]’s buggy** and its occupied seat count. The server assigns the first free passenger seat atomically. The driver alone controls the buggy. Passengers follow its position, heading and slope pitch, with normal movement/jump/vehicle controls disabled. When stopped, use **Get out** or F to leave. A full or moving buggy cannot be boarded, and moving passengers wait for a stop before getting out.

Boarding uses an authenticated `buggy-ride` action on the presence socket, with identity supplied by the verified connection. Clients cannot specify their seat or impersonate another rider. The server checks proximity, activity, outdoors/on-foot state, freshness, stopping and capacity, and rate limits boarding actions. Snapshot seat metadata is validated for legal driver links and duplicate occupancy. Passenger positions derive from the driver's accepted pose, rather than their submitted coordinates. Nearby chat follows that seated position. Driver dismount, relocation, leaving Walk mode, disconnect or a stale connection clears its passenger seats at the previous buggy location; the client chooses a collision-safe walking dismount. Passenger departure frees the seat. Membership and seats are transient within the running shared room, not persistent records.

## Movement and rendering

Bicycles reach 9 m/s (about 32 km/h); buggies reach 12 m/s (about 43 km/h), with reverse capped at 1.4 m/s. Walking ramps at 1.2 m/s² to 2.5 m/s, or 5 m/s with Shift; indoor caps remain 1.15/1.9 m/s. Bicycles and buggies ramp at 1.8 and 2.4 m/s², reaching their caps in about five seconds of full throttle. Key release brakes walking promptly; Space brakes the bicycle at 12 m/s² and the buggy at 22 m/s², retaining roughly 3.5 metres of full-speed braking distance. The server permits the higher outdoor riding speeds only for a validated bicycle or buggy pose in Walk mode and retains the lower walking bound. Vehicles use small swept substeps and overlapping body-clearance disks against buildings, trunks, lamp poles, canals, campus boundaries. Steering checks the new footprint before applying rotation. Rendered road-ribbon triangles are indexed in eight-metre cells for wheel contact, including mitered bends and elevated footpaths. Tyre contact samples the rendered road top, ground and bridge deck; positive front-up pitch follows slopes and clears the wheelbase at grade changes. Roads do not restrict travel. Steering tightens less at higher speeds, the camera follows gently, and vehicle and seat transforms follow road gradients.

The buggy has open sides, two cushioned benches, a canopy, windshield, headlights and rear lamps. Remote passengers render individual seated avatars at their assigned positions; only the driver's renderer creates a buggy body. Models include rolling wheels and bicycle pedalling. Lamps are decorative and add no extra real-time light sources to the nighttime crowd. There is no parked vehicle inventory or driver transfer. Football and explorable interiors stay on foot. Static obstacle collision runs in the client, as it does for walking; authenticated presence retains identity, capacity, speed and origin checks.

Validation covers frame-rate consistency, acceleration/braking/reverse/pause, open-ground travel, tyre clearance on elevated roads, slopes and bends, thin walls/trunks/poles, body clearance during turns, safe mounting/dismounting, real-campus placement, seat capacity/reuse, illegal boarding, position spoofing, tilt bounds, slope transforms and driver departure/relocation. Authenticated WebSocket tests verify live boarding, passenger follow, vehicle changes, and seat cleanup across peers.

## Bump another buggy

Drive into another active buggy to transfer momentum. Faster closing speeds produce stronger pushes; glancing contacts preserve sideways travel and add a small heading kick. Rubber bumpers, an ivory hood stripe and the driver's colour distinguish the buggies. A brief suspension response moves the body while keeping the wheels on the ground. Space brakes recoil as well as driving, and passengers continue following their driver's accepted pose. These impacts affect buggies only.

The presence server detects capsule contact across each accepted movement segment, including heading changes, using bounded observed velocities. It computes a single equal-mass impulse with 0.35 restitution for both drivers. Combined drift and driving remain capped at the shared 12.6 m/s presence allowance; an impact does not refill the movement budget. A conservative broad phase skips distant bodies. Only active, visible, fresh outdoor drivers on similar ground heights participate; mounting or relocating does not create an impact. A pair must separate before another collision can fire, and each update resolves at most one new pair.

The server sends a sequenced impact directly to both drivers and includes it briefly in snapshots for recovery. Clients acknowledge the sequence; old queued poses cannot overwrite a pending impact. Duplicate or older deliveries do not replay recoil. The driving integrator applies bounded drift and torque in its existing small substeps, with full obstacle clearance, and only reconciles to the contact anchor if the intervening route is safe. A short presentation offset smooths the correction without changing the physical collider. Remote visitors retain their buffered interpolation and use the shared impact time for the suspension response. Detailed terrain collision still runs on each driver client; this does not add a fully server-simulated world or guarantee equal latency on all networks.

Tests cover energy/momentum, speed-dependent and glancing hits, swept contact, 30/60/144 fps recoil, braking, obstacle clearance, sustained contact and rearming, 32 buggies with 16 simultaneous pairs, duplicate/delayed snapshot delivery, and authenticated WebSocket delivery with passengers and an observer. `/tests/fixtures/buggy-impact-preview.html` provides a development-only visual review of the production model and solver.
