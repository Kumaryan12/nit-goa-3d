# Football controls and shared simulation

Join from Sports Ground or **Play football**. WASD / thumbstick moves, Shift / Run sprints, arrows / campus drag aim, Q / Pass sends a short pass, and Space / Shoot takes a shot. Running gives a stronger shot; J still jumps on desktop. Phone shot buttons live beside the movement pad, accept non-primary pointers, and suppress duplicate compatibility clicks. Help and manual kickoff are inside **Controls & kickoff**.

Football accelerates at 7 m/s² to the existing 2.5 m/s walking or 5 m/s running cap. Campus walking retains its slower acceleration. Football starts with a higher camera angle, distance 9.5 m, and a ground arrow beside the avatar so the ball and aim are readable. Drag, scroll and pinch remain available. Team admission assigns separate available spawn slots facing the correct goal, including a rotated pitch.

The authenticated server owns ball positions, goals and scores. Fixed 1/60-second physics steps consume a bounded 100 ms catch-up budget; broadcasts remain 20 Hz. Front-facing contact within 1.15 m eases the ball's velocity toward a leading position, using speed measured from accepted poses. It does not teleport the ball or capture shots over 10 m/s. One eligible dribbler per tick is chosen by distance, then stable ID. Shots are 22 m/s, running shots 30 m/s, and passes 11 m/s. Goal posts, swept goal detection, touchlines and kickoff countdowns remain shared.

Kicks carry a validated current direction. Reach is still 1.8 m; a press within 2.6 m can wait at most 180 ms for contact or cooldown. Pause, stale poses, departure, resets and expiry discard buffered presses. Rate, identity, origin, room capacity and movement validation stay on the server. Sequenced pose acknowledgements allow up to two seconds of bounded transport catch-up; the client corrects only after accepted poses stop progressing for 1.5 seconds and divergence exceeds 2 m.

The ball keeps eight snapshots on a shared timeline, renders 75 ms behind it, and predicts at most 100 ms using drag before holding position. Revision changes snap kickoff resets directly to center; goals and out-of-play positions stop prediction. A small visual blend softens packet corrections. This adds bounded display delay and does not remove latency on a poor connection. Remote players retain their existing 100 ms movement buffer and receive accepted kick animations; their per-frame state lookups use an index built on receipt. Automatic and Smooth football keep every opponent visible as a lightweight articulated athlete in team colours, with shared geometry for each hairstyle and four stable player labels. Moving around does not mount and dispose different portrait models. Explicit Detailed graphics retain the nearest 12 full models and use single-draw-call models beyond the detail budget.

Verification includes the full regression suite, frame-rate-consistent football acceleration, jitter/reset/edge rendering, pass and shot validation, buffered-action expiry, contested touches, fresh pose acknowledgement handling, unique spawns, concurrent touch ownership and 24 authenticated loopback WebSocket peers receiving one shared pass. The development-only `/tests/fixtures/football-preview.html` rehearses the real controller, pitch and controls with in-memory room physics and optional 180 ms delayed ball snapshots. It does not connect to production or bypass authentication, and the production server does not serve it. Chrome phone emulation checks layout; a physical two-phone rehearsal remains useful for network and hardware differences.

Goals trigger a team-coloured GOAL banner and an instanced confetti burst near each player's view. The server's score and 2.5-second kickoff countdown determine the celebration identity and age: repeated packets cannot restart it, reconnects do not replay the same score, and late arrivals see only the remaining time. A local deadline clears it even if packets stop. Confetti uses one draw call, 32 pieces in smooth graphics or 64 otherwise, no shadows or collisions, and no per-frame React updates. Reduced-motion visitors receive the static score banner without moving particles or entry animation.

Football automatic graphics cap DPR at .9, shadow resolution at 512 and facade detail distance at 120 m. Stadium shadows are cached; one instanced contact-shadow draw follows all 24 players and the ball. Portrait characters and the ball do not cast into that cached map. Detailed mode retains dynamic shadows and portrait detail. Slow desktop football can reach the .65 DPR recovery tier too. Automatic quality considers 95th-percentile frame times as well as average FPS; idle football cannot promote desktop automatic graphics beyond the balanced tier and trigger a costly scene rebuild as running begins. An explicit Detailed choice keeps its quality settings. The directional shadow camera centres on the pitch with a 64 m half extent, and rendered trees retain a generous 140 m surrounding radius plus canopy allowance. Leaving football restores the full campus tree list and shadow extent. Collision checks always retain the full world.

Camera booms gather conservative tree, building and pole candidates once, then perform the same sampled collision tests. Remote football avatars keep a stable initial position and memoized rendering, so detail/label changes cannot reapply old admission coordinates. Grounded peers no longer sample a second movement buffer just to retrieve jump height. The ball's white shell and black pentagons share one vertex-coloured geometry/material. Football camera following uses rate 14 instead of the campus rate 8, with the same frame-rate-independent exponential easing.

Full-campus rehearsal: add `?full=1&players=24` to the development fixture. It reports two-second average/peak calls and triangles, frame rate and peak actor speed; `Delay packets` adds 180 ms transport delay. In Chrome on the development Mac, a fixed 24-avatar view measured 355 → 165 average draw calls, 629,968 → 304,740 average triangles, and 1,224,718 → 532,997 peak triangles. Running at the verified 5 m/s cap measured 200 → 55 average calls and 419,329 → 155,438 average triangles in sampled windows; both versions reached the display's 60 fps cap. The earlier campus renderer was compared with the same controller and ball changes to isolate rendering work. This is a local rehearsal with in-memory peers, not physical phone hardware or 24 distributed browsers.

A separate seven-run CPU benchmark over 3,000 representative real-campus running steps and two camera checks per frame measured median 613.8 → 85.1 ms, with identical accumulated positions/collision fractions. Regression tests cover large building/courtyard bounds, negative-coordinate trees, pole candidate cost, graphics recovery/restoration and merged ball topology. Full auth, movement, goal and multiplayer validation remain in place.

The grass instance matrices now upload only when their source chunks change, rather than on every graphics-profile change. Football panels retain their translucent colour without continuously blurring the moving canvas. Runtime metrics report 95th-percentile/worst frame times and counts above 34 ms in each two-second window. Add `&moving=1` to the full 24-player fixture to rehearse roaming opponents with validated poses.

Follow-up Chrome stress rehearsal with 4× CPU slowdown and 23 moving in-memory opponents: the deployed renderer sampled 31 fps, 117.6 ms p95 and 405.2 ms worst; the steady renderer sampled 60 fps, 17.7 ms p95 and 19.6 ms worst. These are local sampled windows, not a guarantee for every browser/device or a distributed multiplayer benchmark.
